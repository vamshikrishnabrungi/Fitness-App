# Wrestling Understanding Brief

## Purpose

This brief captures standalone wrestling knowledge before creating research agents or database records. Wrestling should not be treated as MMA grappling. It has its own rule sets, scoring incentives, positions, phases, training culture, technical progressions, conditioning profile, and injury risks.

The future SFTC wrestling database should support:

- scholastic/folkstyle wrestling
- freestyle wrestling
- Greco-Roman wrestling
- beginner-to-advanced skill teaching
- strength and conditioning for wrestling
- competition-week planning
- return-to-wrestling and injury-aware progression
- role/style-specific training such as leg attacker, upper-body thrower, counter wrestler, rider/top wrestler, bottom escape specialist

## Wrestling Styles And Why They Matter

### Folkstyle / Scholastic / Collegiate Wrestling

Folkstyle is common in US school and college wrestling. It rewards control heavily.

Backend implications:

- Top control, riding, mat returns, breakdowns, turns, escapes, reversals, and mat wrestling matter more than in freestyle.
- Bottom wrestling is a major skill area: base building, hand control, stand-ups, switches, granby/roll options, and avoiding riding time/control.
- S&C must support repeated mat returns, hand fighting, top pressure, hip heists, sprawls, and hard isometric positions.

### Freestyle Wrestling

Freestyle rewards takedowns, exposure, turns, and high-amplitude attacks. Control is important, but exposing the opponent's back and scoring quickly from par terre are central.

Backend implications:

- Feet-to-back skills, leg attacks, reattacks, chest wraps, gut wrenches, leg laces, and turn defense matter.
- Athletes need fast transitions after takedown because par terre scoring windows can be short.
- Advanced users need exposure-awareness and edge tactics.

### Greco-Roman Wrestling

Greco-Roman forbids attacking below the waist. It emphasizes upper-body control, pummeling, body locks, arm drags, throws, lifts, par terre offense/defense, and posture.

Backend implications:

- Leg attacks and leg-based takedown entries should be blocked for Greco-specific profiles.
- Pummeling, underhooks, overhooks, two-on-one, body lock, arm throw, head position, hip position, and lift mechanics become central.
- Neck, trunk, grip, upper back, hip extension, and shoulder durability are especially important.

## Core Wrestling Phases

### 1. Neutral Position

The match begins here. Neutral contains:

- stance and motion
- level change
- penetration step
- hand fighting
- collar ties, inside ties, wrists, two-on-one, underhook, overhook
- snaps and front headlock
- single-leg, high-crotch, double-leg, low single, ankle pick, duck under, arm drag
- reattacks
- sprawls
- go-behinds
- edge wrestling

Backend teaching rule:

- Beginners should first learn stance, motion, level change, hand position, penetration step, sprawl, and go-behind before live takedown chaos.
- Intermediate athletes learn chain wrestling: attack, opponent reacts, finish or reattack.
- Advanced athletes learn opponent-specific tie systems, setups, edge tactics, and match-score decisions.

### 2. Top / Par Terre / Control Position

Depending on style, top wrestling includes:

- breakdowns
- rides
- mat returns
- wrist control
- spiral ride, tight waist, ankle ride, claw, legs-in systems
- tilts and turns
- gut wrench, leg lace, lifts in freestyle/Greco
- preventing escapes/reversals

Backend teaching rule:

- Folkstyle users need more top riding, breakdown, turn, mat-return, and control work.
- Freestyle/Greco users need faster turn windows, exposure attacks, and par terre defense.

### 3. Bottom / Defensive Position

Especially important in folkstyle. Includes:

- base building
- hand control
- stand-up
- sit-out
- switch
- hip heist
- granby/roll options
- mat return defense
- turn defense

Backend teaching rule:

- Beginners should learn safe base, hand control, stand-up, hip heist, and belly-down turn defense before complex rolls.
- Advanced bottom wrestling should include specific responses to common rides and mat returns.

### 4. Scramble

Scrambling happens when neither athlete has clean control. It includes:

- whizzer positions
- shin whizzer
- funk rolls
- leg pass
- limp leg
- hip heist
- re-scramble
- stalemate awareness
- exposure-risk decisions

Backend teaching rule:

- Beginners should not be pushed into complex scramble/funk positions before they can safely sprawl, hip heist, and protect knees/neck.
- Intermediate and advanced athletes need scramble decision trees, not random effort.

## Wrestling Technical Pillars

### Stance And Motion

Key ideas:

- balanced stance
- knees bent, hips loaded
- head up, chest over knees
- hands ready
- pressure through feet
- motion without crossing feet
- level change without reaching

Common errors:

- standing tall
- reaching with arms instead of moving feet
- crossing feet
- head down on attack
- shooting from too far
- resting in stance instead of actively controlling position

### Hand Fighting And Ties

Key ties:

- collar tie
- inside tie
- wrist control
- two-on-one/Russian tie
- underhook
- overhook/whizzer
- front headlock
- body lock

Backend logic:

- Hand fighting is not random pushing. It should create angle, level change, reaction, posture break, or positional advantage.
- Beginners learn grip/hand safety and basic tie clearing.
- Intermediate athletes learn tie-to-attack systems.
- Advanced athletes build preferred tie systems by body type, style, and opponent reaction.

### Takedown Offense

Main families:

- leg attacks: single, double, high crotch, low single, sweep single, ankle pick
- upper-body attacks: arm drag, duck under, throw-by, headlock, body lock, lateral drop, arm throw
- snap-down/front-headlock attacks
- trips and foot sweeps
- reattacks

Progression:

- stance and penetration mechanics
- entry without partner
- partner feed
- finish mechanics
- opponent defense
- chain finish
- live go

Beginner gates:

- can level change without knee/back pain
- can keep head/posture safe
- can finish at technical pace
- can fall safely

### Takedown Defense

Main families:

- sprawl
- hips back
- head/hands defense
- whizzer
- crossface
- downblock
- square hips
- circle/go-behind
- limp leg
- shin whizzer
- edge defense

Progression:

- sprawl shape
- sprawl plus circle
- defend one specific shot
- defend chain finish
- reattack
- live short-go

### Top Control And Turns

Top goals:

- control hips and wrists
- break opponent's base
- prevent stand-up/switch
- create turns or exposure
- return opponent to mat

Beginner:

- tight waist, wrist control, basic breakdown, safe pressure

Intermediate:

- mat returns, spiral ride, tilt chains, near-fall/exposure setups

Advanced:

- style-specific ride/turn systems, edge/control tactics, par terre conversion

### Bottom Escapes And Reversals

Bottom goals:

- protect from turns
- build base
- win hand control
- create hip separation
- stand up, sit out, switch, or reverse

Beginner:

- base, hand control, stand-up, hip heist

Intermediate:

- switch, sit-out chains, granby only when safe, mat-return defense

Advanced:

- ride-specific counters, timing against mat returns, score-state bottom decisions

## Wrestling Style Archetypes

These are not fixed identities, but retrieval can use them as role tags.

### Leg Attacker

Needs:

- stance motion
- level change
- penetration step
- single/double/high-crotch families
- chain finishes
- reattacks
- hamstring/adductor/knee/low-back capacity

Risks:

- knee irritation from shots
- neck/back risk from poor head position
- fatigue from repeated failed shots

### Upper-Body / Greco-Oriented Wrestler

Needs:

- pummeling
- underhook/overhook
- body lock
- arm throw
- lift mechanics
- trunk/neck/upper-back/grip strength

Risks:

- shoulder, neck, rib, low-back stress
- throw safety

### Counter Wrestler

Needs:

- stance discipline
- sprawl
- whizzer
- reattack
- front headlock
- go-behind
- edge tactics

Risks:

- passive habits if countering replaces offense
- neck/shoulder stress from front-headlock positions

### Top / Ride Specialist

Needs:

- breakdowns
- wrist control
- hip pressure
- mat returns
- turns
- grip endurance
- trunk/neck strength

Risks:

- elbow/wrist/shoulder overload
- excessive isometric fatigue

### Scrambler

Needs:

- hip mobility
- knee control
- proprioception
- positional awareness
- leg pass/funk/hip heist decisions

Risks:

- knee/ankle/neck positions if introduced too early

## Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced Teaching Model

### Beginner Wrestling

Main goal:

- build safe wrestling literacy and movement.

Teach:

- stance and motion
- level change
- penetration step
- sprawl
- hand fighting basics
- safe falling
- basic single/double finish at technical pace
- basic top pressure and breakdown
- base and stand-up
- simple conditioning without destroying technique

Avoid:

- live throws
- complex scramble/funk positions
- high-volume shots under fatigue
- hard neck bridges
- advanced leg rides or dangerous rolls

Promotion signals:

- can hold stance without collapsing
- can shoot technically without knee/back pain
- sprawls safely
- understands basic score goals
- can build base and stand up
- no worsening neck/knee/shoulder/back symptoms

### Intermediate Wrestling

Main goal:

- connect attacks, defense, top/bottom, and match situations.

Teach:

- tie-to-attack systems
- chain wrestling
- reattacks
- mat returns
- ride/turn chains
- bottom escape chains
- short live goes
- style-specific emphasis
- energy-system conditioning

Avoid:

- making every practice live
- high-volume shots when knees/back are irritated
- complex throws without breakfall and partner control

Promotion signals:

- links at least two attacks
- defends and reattacks
- can ride or escape against resistance
- manages match score/time
- maintains technique under fatigue

### Advanced Wrestling

Main goal:

- individualize systems by style, ruleset, opponent, weight class, competition calendar, and score state.

Teach:

- opponent-specific tie systems
- edge tactics
- par terre conversion
- advanced mat returns
- high-level scramble decisions
- match strategy by period/score
- taper and competition-week sharpness
- strength/power maintenance

Avoid:

- late-week novelty
- overloading live goes close to competition
- unmanaged weight-cut and dehydration
- ignoring pain/readiness signals

## Wrestling S&C Needs

Wrestling needs:

- high relative strength
- repeated explosive shots and sprawls
- isometric strength in ties and rides
- grip endurance
- neck and trunk stiffness
- hip mobility and strength
- posterior-chain strength
- adductor and groin capacity
- knee and ankle robustness
- aerobic base for recovery
- anaerobic repeat effort for flurries and scrambles

Useful training categories:

- squat/lunge/hinge/carry/pull/push patterns
- sled push/pull when available
- loaded carries
- rope climbs or towel-grip pulling when appropriate
- med-ball throws
- sprawls and shot mechanics at controlled volumes
- trunk anti-rotation and anti-extension
- neck isometrics when safe and appropriate
- mobility for hips, ankles, thoracic spine, shoulders

Bad programming:

- random conditioning circuits that ruin wrestling mechanics
- high-volume shots with knee/back pain
- hard neck fatigue before live wrestling
- heavy lower-body maxing before competition
- weight-cut driven hard conditioning

## Competition Week Logic

Competition week should:

- preserve speed and sharpness
- reduce soreness
- reduce live volume
- avoid new technique
- protect weight management and hydration
- keep stance, motion, hand fighting, first attacks, and mat returns sharp
- include mobility and recovery

Avoid:

- heavy eccentric lower-body work
- high-volume live wrestling
- new throws/scrambles
- hard conditioning tests
- aggressive dehydration strategies

## Injury And Risk Management

Common risk areas:

- neck
- shoulder
- elbow
- wrist/fingers
- ribs
- low back
- hip/groin/adductors
- knee
- ankle
- skin infections
- concussion/head contact in some contexts
- dehydration/weight-cut risk

Backend safety rules:

- Hold or regress when neck, neurological, severe head, or radiating symptoms appear.
- Reduce shot volume and live wrestling with knee, hip, groin, or low-back irritation.
- Reduce pummeling/throws with shoulder, rib, neck, or back irritation.
- Do not prescribe unsafe weight-cut tactics.
- Emphasize hygiene reminders for wrestling environments, but do not give medical treatment instructions.
